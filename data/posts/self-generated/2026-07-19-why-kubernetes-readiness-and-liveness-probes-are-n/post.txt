Spent two hours yesterday chasing why a freshly deployed pod kept getting killed during startup. The app was fine—logs looked normal, metrics were healthy—but the pod never stayed running long enough to actually serve traffic.

Turned out I'd copied a liveness probe config from another service and used the same settings for readiness. Both checked the same `/health` endpoint every 5 seconds with the same failure threshold. Seemed reasonable at the time.

What I missed: the app needed a solid 45 seconds to load its config and warm up connections. The liveness probe didn't care about that. It just saw 5 consecutive failed checks and assumed the pod was dead, so kubelet killed it. Over and over.

The readiness probe would've been the right gate—keep traffic away until the app's actually ready. Liveness is supposed to catch hung processes, not slow startups. I had them doing the same job.

I fixed it by setting `initialDelaySeconds: 60` on the liveness probe and using a lighter endpoint for it. Readiness stays strict because it actually needs to reflect whether the service can handle requests.

I'm still wrapping my head around whether there's a cleaner pattern here. These two probes seem to always fight each other on timing. Anyone else constantly tweaking these values trying to find the sweet spot?
